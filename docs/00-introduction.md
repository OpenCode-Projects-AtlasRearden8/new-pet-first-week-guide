# 00 · Introduction

Welcome. If you're reading this, you're about to do something quietly wonderful:
give a rescue cat or dog their first real home in a while — maybe ever.

This guide covers **your first week together**. That's it. Week one is when
everything is new to them and (honestly) a little new to you too. It's the week
of hiding under beds, skipped meals, 3 a.m. pacing, and the first tiny stretch
when they finally lie down near you. We'll walk through it one calm step at a
time.

## The one rule, up front

This guide is **education only — it is never veterinary advice.**

- We'll tell you what's *typical* for a newly rescued animal.
- We will never tell you that what you're seeing is *fine*.
- When in doubt, call your vet. A phone call to a clinic is cheap; a missed
  emergency is not.

A newly adopted animal can hide illness for days (instinct says "don't look
weak"). So an animal that seems "just shy" might also be unwell. Trust your
gut, and lean on your vet for anything medical.

## The 3-3-3 rule

Rescue folks often describe adjustment in threes:

- **First 3 days** — your pet is likely overwhelmed. They may hide, not eat
  much, and seem shut-down. This is common.
- **First 3 weeks** — they start to learn your routine and show more of their
  real personality. Some quirks appear now.
- **First 3 months** — they mostly stop auditioning for their old life and
  accept this one. Trust and habits settle in.

**The 3-3-3 rule is a rough pattern, not a promise.** Some pets relax on day
one; some take months. Neither means you're doing it wrong.

## A note on tone

Your new pet is not broken, dramatic, or ungrateful. They're an animal whose
whole world changed two days ago. Kindness and predictability are the entire
job this week. Everything else — Training, progress, cuddles — comes later.

You're doing better than you think.
