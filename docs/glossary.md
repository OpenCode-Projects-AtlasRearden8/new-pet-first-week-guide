# Glossary

Plain-English definitions for the words shelters, rescues, and vet visits
throw around. Education only.

## Adoption and paperwork

**Adoption contract** — the agreement you sign with the rescue; it can carry
requirements (like an initial vet visit). **Surrender notes** — whatever is
known about a pet's history before the rescue; even one line helps your vet.
**Microchip** — a tiny ID chip injected under the skin; scanners read its
number, and you *register* the number with a registry. **Foster** — someone
who temp-pets an animal for the rescue until a permanent home is found; fosters
often know behavior nobody else records. **Return policy** — what to do (and
in what window) if the adoption can't continue; read this in door-week, not
week three.

## Cat people

**FeLiV / FIV testing** — blood tests shelter-run to check for two
contagious cat viruses (feline leukemia virus and feline immunodeficiency
virus). Results matter for existing-cat households; ask what was done.
**Hiding** — a shelter cat's default spreadsheet. See [chapter
4](04-week-one-behavior.md). **Zoomies** — nocturnal sprint sessions. Not a
symptom; a hobby. **Hepatic lipidosis** — a serious liver condition that
fasting can contribute to in cats; why cat fasting is a vet call, not a
"tough night." **Urinary blockage** — most urgent of the week-one cat
emergencies, seen classically in male cats: straining with little or no
urine produced. Hours matter; go now. **Upper respiratory infection (URI)**
— the "shelter cold": sneezing, runny eyes/nose. Common in shelter
environments; mild cases have a wait-and-see path, worsening ones get a day.
**Stranger danger** — the thing your new cat will have with guests forever,
until they don't.

## Dog people

**Heartworm status / prevention** — dogs get tested for heartworm
(parasites transmitted by mosquitoes); "on prevention" means monthly. Rescues
often start treatment before adoption or leave the timeline to you — ask.
**GDV / bloat** — a life-threatening emergency in large, deep-chested dogs
(great danes, shepherds, boxers): a rapidly swelling, hard belly with
unproductive retching. Hours matter. **Velcro dog** — the shadow that
follows you; common in week one and settles. **Resource guarding (RG)** —
growling around food or objects when approached. Don't punish the growl —
it's the warning you *want* to hear before a snap. See [scary
behaviors](08-decoding-scary-behaviors.md).
**Crate training** — teaching the crate as a safe den; a tool you can love
or skip. **Marking** — small pee deposits with opinions attached. Common in
intact males, also in females; potty-training conversation, not a betrayal.
**Loose leash** — walking without pulling. A week-one *goal*, not usually a
week-one *achievement*.

## Vet visits and medicine

**Fecal test** — lab screening for intestinal parasites; done commonly on
new rescues even without symptoms. **Vaccines / boosters** — shots, some
initial series, boosters timed later; your vet maps the schedule from the
rescue's records. **Deworming** — parasite treatment; often given at the
shelter by weight and repeated. **Baseline bloodwork** — lab tests taken
when an animal is apparently healthy, to have a "normal" to compare later.
**Board / behaviorist** — a certified trainer or veterinary behaviorist is
the professional for deeper behavioral patterns (guarding, fear aggression,
persistent house-soiling). Trainers train; *veterinary behaviorists are
vets* who also handle medication. Ask your vet for the referral.
**Wait-and-see** — a legitimate tool, when you set a deadline. See
[chapter 9](09-call-the-vet-or-wait.md). **Emergency animal hospital** —
open at 2 a.m. Find yours before week one begins; that's the one piece of
preparedness you can't get later.

## Every day

**3-3-3 rule** — the rough adjustment arc: 3 days anxious, 3 weeks
settling, 3 months home. [Chapter 0](00-introduction.md). **Enzymatic
cleaner** — the cleaner that digests the stain's proteins, removing the
scent that invites repeats. **Safe room** — one quiet room that belongs to
the new pet until they outgrow it. The whole manual starts here.
