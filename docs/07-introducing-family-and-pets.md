# 07 · Introducing Family (and Existing Pets)

Everyone in the house — human, or previously established animal — is bursting
to meet the newcomer. That excitement is exactly the thing to manage. Week one
belong to calm introductions, not family photos taken an hour after arrival.

*The forever rule:* **the new pet sets the pace.** A meeting that takes 10
minutes and 15 tries beats a 10-minute meeting plus one setback.

## People first

### The core household

- **First 24 hours: nobody but the one "safe person"** who took pickup day.
  Everyone else sees them from the doorway of the safe room at most.
- Then rotate visitors in, quietly, two at a time, for a few minutes each:
  enter, sit low, talk softly, don't approach, and toss a treat from a
  distance. Reader: ten minutes is plenty.
- Ask kids to practice **"flat hand only"** — offer the back of one resting
  hand the way you'd offer food, and never reach. If they can't be that flat
  and slow, they wait via the doorway.

### Kids

- Rule: **flat hand only, no grabbing, no chasing, no waking up.**
- Even friendly dogs can nip when startled from sleep; this isn't a
  breed or "bad dog" trait, it's reflex. Sleep is precious — *do not bother
  the sleeping pet* is the safest line in every house.
- Nothing beats an adult in the room, modeling calm, and saying "we go slow."
  Young children and pets get one adult supervising, always.

### Guests and visitors

- **Week one: no guests to meet the new pet. Full stop.** Household members
  only. The first weekend is plenty busy with calm humans; save the loving
  chaos for month one, and give your pet TWO weekends to get comfortable
  before it starts feeling like an audition they didn't sign up for.
- If someone *must* come over (delivery, landlord), keep the pet in the safe
  room. It's not forever. It's one week.

## Meeting the existing pets — later, not this week

This is the section many people want to skip. Don't. New pets can carry
things (parasites, upper respiratory cat stuff) that existing pets don't
have immunity to, and stressed animals guard resources. Time and separation
protect everyone.

### The waiting room rule (days 1–7+)

- The new pet stays in the safe room. The existing pets live their normal
  lives elsewhere.
- **They know about each other.** Scent travels: swap bedding one day, rub a
  cloth on one pet's cheek and leave it near the other. This quiet scent
  exchange does more than any ten-minute face-off ever will.
- Feed them on opposite sides of the same door — new pet inside, existing
  pet outside — at dinner time. This is the classic positive-association
  trick: *your new roommate smells like dinner.*

### When you do meet in person

1. **Dog–cat meetings:** dog on leash, perfectly neutral fancy leash-walking;
   cat somewhere high and free to leave. If the cat is uncomfortable, the
   meeting is over — that's data, not failure. Many families take two weeks
   of this before real proximity happens. That's normal.
2. **Dog–dog meetings:** first meeting on neutral territory (a sidewalk away
   from home) with two humans, both dogs on loose-ish leashes, parallel
   walking — *not* face-to-face. Wagging and sniffing far apart is the goal
   this week. Real "friends" come month one.
3. **Cat–cat meetings:** the classic slow method — days of door-feeding
   (the trick above), then brief visual contact through a crack or barrier with
   food present, then short supervised visits in the same room. Hissing
   through a door is normal; **working through it in a sealed room, food
   present, day by day, is the entire protocol.** Weeks, not days.

### Signs to slow way down

- Pinned-back ears and prolonged freeze in either animal
- Repeated retreat to hiding after each dog's look
- Guarding behavior over food bowls, toys, or the best windowsill
- Raised hackles persisting, stiff posture and escalating barking

"Slow down" means going back one step for a few days, not abandoning the
plan. If conflicts escalate rather than reduce, a trainer or veterinary
behaviorist's early consult is cheaper than months of drift.

## One more time, the order of operations

1. New pet has the safe room. (Days 1–3)
2. Household humans rotate through, calm and boring. (Days 2–5)
3. Kids meet with adult supervision, flat hands only. (Days 3–5)
4. Scent exchange begins with existing pets — door feeding, bedding swaps. (Days 4–7)
5. Vet visit happens before in-person pet introductions if the rescue/vet
   recommends it (many do). (Day 3–7)
6. First in-person pet meetings, controlled and short — week two, often.

Slow is fast. Rushed is slower.
