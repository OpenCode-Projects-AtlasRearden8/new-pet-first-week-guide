# 04 · Week One Behavior

Week one is a psychology class you didn't sign up for. Here's what's normal,
so you don't misread your new pet — and a short list of the things that
*aren't* normal (then, go to [call the vet or wait?](09-call-the-vet-or-wait.md)).

## The ARC: how adjustment usually flows

Most rescue pets move through stages:

| Stage | Days (roughly) | What it looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A — Anxious** | 1–3 | Hiding, not eating much, shutdown, pacing, clinginess, no play |
| **R — Relaxed-ish** | 3–5 | Eating normally, sleeps expanding, watching you more, mild play |
| **C — Curious** | 5–7 | Exploring, following you, testing boundaries, showing odd new habits |

Some pets sprint through this in two days. Some stall at A for a week. The
*direction* of travel matters more than the schedule.

## Common week-one behaviors and what they mean

### Hiding (both species; cats especially)

Normal for days 1–3+ in cats. A hiding cat that **eats, drinks, and uses the
box when unsupervised** is a cat doing exactly what scared rescue cats do:
stay invisible, act when the coast is clear. Don't drag them out. Sit nearby,
offer treats tossed at a distance, wait.

> Worth checking within a few days: that the food is actually being eaten,
> water dipped into, and litter visited. The [first-week log
> sheet](checklists/first-week-log-sheet.md) exists for exactly this.

### Not eating (anorexia doesn't mean "would like to be thin")

A rescue pet skipping a few meals in the first 48ish hours is common. But
*extended* fasting is dangerous, especially for cats (fasting can contribute
to hepatic lipidosis, a serious liver condition). General education-only
guideposts people often cite:

- Cat: no real eating beyond ~24–48 h → call your vet.
- Dog: no food and no water beyond ~24 h → call your vet.
- Puppy or kitten: these are small engines; they crash fast. Call the
  same-day if they skip meals entirely.

Whether to tempt with food: most folks try gentle things — warming wet food
slightly, offered from your hand, sleeping near the bowl — but if it doesn't
work quickly, that's not stubbornness, that's data.

### Hiding their symptoms — the quiet truth

Sick animals hide. So "my cat hides all day" and "my cat is sick" can look
identical on day one. The log sheet is your tie-breaker: if hiding comes with
skipped meals, no litter/potty use, hiding behavior but no sleeping — that
combination moves from "shy" to "call."

### Zoomies at 2 a.m.

Normal, especially dogs. New environments, new smells, crated hours, and LOUD
naps build energy. Doesn't mean anything is wrong. Doesn't mean they're
unsettled forever. It's 2 a.m. energy, the universal constant.

### Accidents in the house

Very normal in week one. Nerves, new territory, unclear potty patterns. Clean
with enzymatic cleaner (regular soaps leave marks that invite repeats). For
dogs, more frequent trips this week beats holding it in; for cats, confirm the
box location is quiet and reachable, and scoop daily.

> Persistent house soiling — suddenly, in the same spot, with straining or
> crying — is a [vet-call flag](09-call-the-vet-or-wait.md), not bad manners.

### Escaping to under furniture is not "hating you"

They're processing. Two weeks from now, that same cat will be sitting on your
keyboard. Predictability, food on schedule, calm hands, patience. That's the
whole recipe.

### Following you to every room (dog version)

Common in week one, especially in the "clingy dog" arc — sometimes called
velcro behavior. Under it is relief at being attached to you. It settles as
they learn you come back. Leave them briefly with a treat; return without
drama. That's real calming work.

## New quirks that show up in week one

- Resource guarding (growling over bowls, treats, rest spots)
- Sleep suddenly *in* the crate/bed even when the door's open again

Don't panic-fix these yet. Note them in your log; address with training later,
with a trainer if guarding escalates. Week one is for observation, not
correction.

## Red flags — do not wait these out

The full triage page is [chapter 9](09-call-the-vet-or-wait.md). The headline
ones:

- Not eating or drinking (see time guideposts above)
- Straining to urinate, especially male cats
- Repeated vomiting, or any blood — call now, don't photograph and wait
- Lethargy combined with other flags (no food + not drinking + very sleepy)
- Difficulty breathing, pale or bluish gums, collapse: emergency
- Seizures, even brief ones

If you can't tell which side of the line you're on, that's what the phone is
for. Call your vet's office or an emergency line and let a professional
triage. **Calling is never wrong.**
