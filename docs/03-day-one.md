# 03 · Day One

Pickup day, step by step. If today goes sideways — hiding, refusal to
explore, one dramatic fecal event — you're not doing it wrong. That *is* a
normal day one.

## The ride home

- Bring a carrier for cats and small dogs; a second human to sit with a big
  dog is better than letting them roam the car.
- Cover a cat carrier loosely with a blanket. Darkness calms.
- Keep talk minimal and voices low. No radio blaring, no "who's a good boy" at
  volume 11. Car rides are already a lot.
- Go **straight home.** No pet-store victory lap, no visiting relative. Errands
  can wait.

## First hours, in order

1. **One room only.** Depend on the safe room from
   [chapter 2](02-the-safe-room.md). Open the carrier inside that room and
   then *step back*.
2. **Open the door and leave it open.** Don't dump them out, don't pull them
   out. A rescue animal decides when to exit the carrier. They will, when
   you're boring enough.
3. **Show them where food and water are, then leave.** Quietly point, set
   bowls down, and go sit far away in the same room. Do not hover.
4. **Don't expect eating.** A new cat may not eat for 24 hours and still be
   fine. A dog refusing *all* food and water for more than ~24 hours is worth
   a call to your vet.
5. **Low chatter, slow movements.** This first day, they're learning one
   lesson: *this place has nothing alarming in it.*
6. **Potty breaks happen.** For dogs, take them out on leash the moment you
   arrive — before anything else — and stay calm even if they just sniff for
   ten minutes. Reward quietly when something happens.

## What "normal" looks like on day one

- Hiding behind the toilet, under the bed, in the back of the closet
- Wide, dilated eyes; flattened ears; crouched walk
- Not eating much, or eating one bite and retreating
- Cats: possibly no litter use on day one (usually fine; see below)
- Dogs: pacing, panting when it isn't hot, clinginess or total shutdown

**None of these, by themselves, means something's wrong.** They mean your
animal's nervous system is working exactly as designed.

## Three things NOT to do today

1. **Don't invite the family over.** Today is strangers-free. Everyone meets
   later (see [chapter 7](07-introducing-family-and-pets.md)).
2. **Don't bathe them.** They just had their whole world upended; a bath can
   wait, unless the rescue says it's needed for a medical reason.
3. **Don't keep "checking on them" every 5 minutes.** Peek, confirm they're
   breathing and safe, then go live your life. Your being relaxed is the
   lesson.

## About litter box silence (cats)

A cat not using the litter box in the first day is common. Keep the box
clean, keep it finding easy, and give it 24–48 hours. If a cat strains to
urinate, cries in the box, or repeatedly enters without producing anything —
especially a male cat — **that's a same-day vet call**, not a wait-and-see.

## Sleep tonight

Expect restlessness. Some pets won't settle until they've verified the room is safe. Leave a light on near the safe room, put an old t-shirt with your
scent near their bed, and let them sleep wherever they choose tonight.
Tomorrow you can nudge routine. Today, safety.

## Your one job this week

Say this out loud, because it's the thesis of the whole guide:

> "My job this week is to be boringly safe — not to be loved yet."

Pets bond with safe, predictable people. The love part arrives on its own.
