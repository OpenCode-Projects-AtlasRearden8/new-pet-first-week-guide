# 09 · Call the Vet — or Wait?

The question every new rescue parent asks at 11 p.m. This chapter is a calm
triage aid for **education only**. It doesn't diagnose; it tells you *what
kinds of signs people usually call about* — and reminds you that when you're
unsure, the right move is a phone call, and that's never an overreaction.

> **Print the quick table** at the end of this chapter. Tape it near the pet
> food. That's the whole reason this page exists.

## The three categories

### Go now / emergency — don't wait, don't finish the chapter

Difficulty breathing or open-mouth breathing in a *resting* cat; collapse;
seizures; suspected poison (or things like: chocolate, xylitol gum, lily
plants, antifreeze, human meds); major bleeding; a male cat straining or
crying in the litter box with nothing produced (a urinary blockage is a
medical emergency and hours matter); a known or suspected swallowed object
(string in cats, socks in dogs — **never pull visible string out**, it can
be anchored internally); pale or bluish gums; bloated hard belly in a large
deep-chested dog (possible GDV/bloat — hours matter); hit by car or a fall;
heatstroke signs; newborn/very young kitten or puppy that's cold, weak, or
not nursing.

If your vet is closed: **every area has an emergency animal hospital.** Save
its address and phone in your phone the day you adopt. This is the single
best preparation for week one.

### Call today — vet same-day or same-shift decides

Not eating (beyond a day or so — see time guideposts below); drinking
extremely little or an enormous amount; repeated vomiting; diarrhea lasting
more than a day, or any blood in stool/urine; persistent coughing or
sneezing; limping or refusing to bear weight; a wound; swelling anywhere;
squinting or a cloudy eye; a feverish-feeling warm animal with
grinding-of-teeth posture; a painful yelp when touched or lifted; new
extreme lethargy that lasts beyond "they're just tired"; a bite or scratch
even when your pet seems fine afterward (infection sets in quietly).

### Reasonable to wait-and-see (with a deadline)

Skipped meal on day one; hiding with normal eating/drinking/potty; one soft
stool after a stressful day; mild snorting right after excitement; occasional
sneezing with no discharge; grooming a lot right after a car ride; midnight
zoomies. Set yourself a mental deadline: "if this isn't clearly better or
clearly gone by tomorrow afternoon, I call."

## Time guideposts people ask about

- **Cat, off food ~24–48 h** → call the vet now, don't "wait it out." Cats
  fast badly.
- **Dog, off food AND water ~24 h** → call the vet now.
- **Kitten or puppy skipping meals** → same-day call. Small animals burn
  through reserves fast.
- **Something new for 2+ days in a row** → it's crossed from "quirk" to
  "worth a call."

## The wait-and-watch checklist (for the gray zone)

If you're genuinely in the "wait" category, watch like a scientist:

1. **Note the start time.** Precision beats vibes.
2. **Check three vitals-ish things:** are they eating? drinking? using the
   bathroom normally?
3. **Log every 2–4 hours** on the [first-week log
   sheet](checklists/first-week-log-sheet.md): symptom, food, water, energy,
   sleep.
4. **Set a deadline.** "If X hasn't improved by 6 p.m., I call." Write it
   down. ADHD-friendly tip: an actual phone alarm.
5. **Change one variable at a time.** Not food + water + bedding on the same
   hour; you'll never know what mattered.

## If you have to call

Describe what you *observed*, not what you conclude: "He's eaten nothing
since 6 a.m. yesterday, drank twice, vomited twice — once at 7 p.m. and once
at 9 p.m.," rather than "he's really sick." Your [log
sheet](checklists/first-week-log-sheet.md) IS this information, ready-made.

Have these handy: the pet's age and weight (estimate is fine), current
medications, the rescue's medical records, and your vet's number.

## The pocket table

| Sign | Category |
| --- | --- |
| Trouble breathing, collapse, seizure, pale gums | **Go now** |
| Male cat straining in box, nothing produced | **Go now** |
| Bloated hard belly in a big dog | **Go now** |
| Swallowed string/object, bleeding, suspected poison | **Go now** |
| Didn't eat past ~24–48 h (cat) | **Call today** |
| Repeated vomiting, blood in stool/urine, limping | **Call today** |
| Persistent cough/sneeze with discharge; squinty eye | **Call today** |
| Skipped one meal, day one | Wait-and-see, deadline set |
| Hiding but eating/potty normally | Wait-and-see, deadline set |
| One odd stool, then normal | Wait-and-see, deadline set |
| Something "off" for 2+ days straight | **Call today** |

## One more reframe

New rescue parents routinely *under*-call in week one because "it's probably
adjustment," then over-call in month six. Flip that: in week one, err toward
calling. Your vet expects new-pet-parent calls. They exist for this.
