# 01 · Before You Bring Them Home

A little prep before pickup day removes half the stress of week one. You don't
need everything — you need the essentials in the right room.

## The short shopping list

**For a dog:**

- Leash (a plain 6-foot one) and flat collar or harness with ID tag
- Food and water bowls
- The same food the shelter/rescue was feeding (sudden food swaps cause upsets)
- A crate or exercise pen if you plan to crate-train
- Pee pads or poop bags
- A couple of chew-safe toys and something soft to sleep on

**For a cat:**

- A litter box (one per cat, plus one extra, ideally) and unscented litter
- Food and water bowls, placed away from the litter box
- The same food they've been eating
- A carrier — you need it for day one *and* every vet visit
- A scratching post or pad
- Hides: a cardboard box, a covered bed, or a quiet closet corner

**For both:**

- Baby gates or a way to block off rooms
- Enzymatic cleaner for accidents (regular cleaner leaves smells they revisit)
- Your phone charged, and the rescue's phone number saved

## What you typically get from the rescue

Paperwork varies, but ask for these at pickup (many rescues send them by
email — ask where they are):

- **Adoption paperwork** — the contract, adoption date, and fee receipt
- **Medical records** — vaccines given, deworming dates, any treatments; for
  dogs, heartworm status; for cats, FeLiV/FIV test results if done
- **Microchip number** and the registration company
- **Surrender/history notes** — even a scrap like "found on Highway 9" helps
  your vet build a picture
- **The exact food they're on** — the brand, formula, and any schedule
- **A feeding amount and schedule**, if the rescue provided one

If something is missing, ask before you leave. Rescues are busy; follow-up
emails are normal, but "I'll get it to you" day-of is worth a gentle push.

## Tip: photograph everything

Snap photos or scans of the paperwork into a folder on your computer the
day you get home. Paper gets lost; week one will be busy; your vet's office on
day 3 will ask for exactly this.

## One thing *not* to buy yet

Don't buy a big haul of new gear based on guesses about what your pet "will
want." Rescue animals often ignore the expensive bed and adore the $2
cardboard box from your last delivery. Give them a few days; buy when their
preferences surprise you (they will).
